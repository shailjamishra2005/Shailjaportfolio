The Brief

Build a website that communicates who you are before you say a word.

The assignment was simple: create a personal portfolio website. The actual challenge was much harder — deciding what a first-year student with no formal work experience should communicate to the world, and then building something that communicated it.

Every decision on this site is a branding decision. The colour palette signals something. The typography signals something. The way the navigation is written signals something. This project was where I first understood that personal branding is not about self-promotion. It is about intentional communication.

Design Decisions

Every colour has a reason. Every font has a reason.

Colour palette

Botanical sage green

Sage green reads as calm, considered and slightly unconventional. It signals someone who takes aesthetics seriously without trying too hard — which is exactly the personality I wanted the site to project.

Display typeface

Fraunces — a variable serif

Fraunces is literary and distinctive without being precious. It was originally designed for a brand with strong values and a clear voice. That resonated with what I was building.

Body typeface

Jost — a geometric sans

Clean, modern and readable at small sizes. It contrasts the warmth of Fraunces with structure and clarity — the two qualities I wanted the site to balance.

Technology choice

Pure HTML and CSS, no frameworks

Building without frameworks meant understanding every line of what I was writing. It was slower but the learning was in the friction.

What I Learned

Building this taught me more about brand strategy than any textbook.

i.

Constraints are a creative tool

Working in pure HTML and CSS with no design tools or frameworks forced clarity. When you cannot rely on a plugin to make something look good, you have to actually understand why it looks good.

ii.

Personal branding is positioning

Deciding what to include was harder than building the site. What you leave out is as much a brand decision as what you put in. Every portfolio is an argument for why someone should care about you.

iii.

Typography is tone of voice

Two websites with identical content but different fonts communicate completely different things. Typeface choice is not decoration. It is personality made visible.

Key Takeaway

This project was the first time I understood that marketing is not something you do to a product after it is made. It is embedded in every decision about how the product is built, presented and experienced. A portfolio website is a marketing problem first and a technical problem second.
